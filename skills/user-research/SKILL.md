# User research

Connect a product decision to a research question and choose evidence that can answer it. Separate what participants did, what they said and what the researcher inferred.

## Plan the study

1. Identify the decision, existing evidence, uncertainty and target audience. Turn solution assumptions into questions: ask how people currently recover a lost draft, rather than whether they want a recovery button.
2. Match the method to the question. Interviews explore experiences and explanations; observation and usability tasks reveal behaviour in context; surveys estimate reported attitudes when sampling supports it; analytics locate patterns without necessarily explaining causes. Do not claim interviews prove demand or a usability session predicts conversion.
3. Define relevant participant characteristics, inclusion needs, recruitment limitations and accommodations. Justify sample and rounds against the decision and diversity of contexts rather than asserting a universal participant count.
4. Draft a guide, task materials, observation plan and analysis approach. Define what evidence would support or challenge the design. Pilot when practical and revise leading questions or unclear tasks.

Use [research methods](references/methods.md) for interview and usability guide structures and [synthesis](references/synthesis.md) for interpreting supplied evidence.

## Work with people and data

Planning does not authorise contacting participants, sending invitations, recording sessions or uploading private material. Follow existing consent, retention and access arrangements for authorised research. Collect only needed information, use participant identifiers in outputs and avoid including identifying details in quotations. Flag when the task needs expertise or access beyond what is available.

A model-generated persona, role-play or predicted answer is a hypothesis, never a participant or a research result. Do not invent interviews, quotations, demographic coverage or findings from inaccessible transcripts.

## Deliverables

For planning, provide the research question, decision, method, participant criteria, guide/tasks, observations to capture and limitations. For synthesis, provide evidence-linked findings, contradictory cases, confidence and limits, design implications and unanswered questions. Use participant/session identifiers and timestamps or source locations when supplied. Missing evidence remains missing.

Keep research synthesis distinct from engineering delivery verification: evidence-synthesis assesses implemented/merged/deployed claims; this skill interprets user evidence. Use design-critique to coordinate a broader interface review.
