# Research guides

These structures are toolkit recommendations; adapt them to the question, audience and existing research practice.

## Interview guide

- Opening: purpose, participation arrangements and permission to skip questions.
- Context: role, relevant circumstances and a recent concrete experience.
- Walk-through: what happened, what they used, what was difficult and how they handled it.
- Neutral probes: ask for examples and clarification; avoid praising answers or suggesting the desired feature.
- Close: missing topics and next steps under the agreed research process.

Prefer a recent incident over hypothetical purchasing intent. Separate a participant's explanation from observed behaviour. Pilot the guide for clarity and flow.

Method source: [GOV.UK in-depth interviews](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews).

## Usability guide

Choose a plausible task, starting state and completion condition. Give the goal without naming the interface route. Capture completion, consequential errors, recovery, requests for help and where the moderator intervened. Record prompts and assistance because they change interpretation. Do not label an assisted completion unassisted.

Example: ask someone to change the destination for an upcoming delivery, rather than telling them to open Settings and select Edit address. Explain limits of the prototype before the session without teaching the route being evaluated.

Method source: [GOV.UK moderated usability testing](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing).

## Study planning

Keep the question tied to a decision and leave time to analyse evidence before the next round. State participant coverage, tool limitations, prototype fidelity and accessibility accommodations. A study on a desktop prototype cannot establish touch or screen-reader usability.

Planning source: [GOV.UK planning a round](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-round-of-user-research).
